Kyiv · February 20, 2014

Who killed people on Kyiv’s Maidan on February 20, 2014— Claude’s analysis

On February 20, 2014, 48 protesters were killed on and near Instytutska Street, which leads from the Maidan to the government quarter, almost all of them in a single morning. In 2023 a court attributed most of the deaths to the Kyiv Berkut. The political scientist Ivan Katchanovski claims the opposite: that people were killed by shooters from the Maidan itself. The analysis on this page was done by Claude, an AI model: it checked both versions against each of the 48 deaths.

In Claude’s estimate, roughly 28 of the 48 deaths are attributable to the Berkut special company, about 11 to other security forces, and about 9 to fire from the Maidan side.

The case and the dispute

A verdict that has not yet entered into force

On October 18, 2023, the Sviatoshynskyi District Court of Kyiv delivered its verdict on five former officers of the Kyiv Berkut. One of them, the deputy regiment commander, was sentenced in absentia to life imprisonment. Two were sentenced to 15 years for killings committed while carrying out a manifestly criminal order, and one to five years for exceeding authority. One was acquitted.

The court found the convicted men involved in 35 of the 48 deaths. For two deaths it pointed directly to the Hotel Ukraina sector. In December 2019 all the defendants were handed over to territory not controlled by Ukraine in an exchange of detainees. The appeal is still under way, with no decision.

The verdict’s main critic is Ivan Katchanovski, a political scientist at the University of Ottawa. In his book “The Maidan Massacre in Ukraine” (Palgrave Macmillan, 2024) he claims that most or nearly all of the victims were killed by organized shooters on the Maidan side in a false-flag operation carried out by part of the protest leadership.

Claude went through all 48 deaths and asked the same question about each: whose shot is more probable, taking into account everything that is known and everything Katchanovski disputes.The percentages in this text are Claude’s estimates based on the case record, not established facts.

The day before

The morning of February 20 did not come out of nowhere. Two days earlier, the protest had turned into an exchange of fire for the first time.

  1. February 18
    A march to the Rada, a dispersal, and in the evening an assault on the Maidan. In one day 19 protesters and 8 members of the security forces died; 120 and 133 people, respectively, received gunshot wounds. The court writes that both sides used violence, including the covert use of firearms.
  2. February 18, daytime
    The Minister of Internal Affairs, the head of the SBU and other officials decide that the security forces will use their authority to employ weapons. The special company of the Kyiv Berkut regiment receives rifles, pistols and pump-action shotguns.
  3. February 19
    The confrontation subsides. On Instytutska Street, past the intersection with Olhynska Street, cranes put concrete blocks in place: by evening a barricade up to three meters high is ready. Late in the evening a truce is announced.
  4. the night before the 20th
    Since the night of February 18–19, the special company has been held in reserve by the October Palace; the officers wear yellow tape on their sleeves to identify one another. According to the cameras, the night passes relatively quietly.

By morning both sides knew that the other side was firing live ammunition.

08:59:54

A Berkut group with green markings fires from behind the parapet above the footbridge. This is not the Kyiv special company: the court considers it the Crimean Berkut, the prosecution the company from Sevastopol. Twenty seconds before this frame, Heorhii Arutiunian was killed by a buckshot round at the footbridge; the court established that this group fired the shot.

05:30The first shots: at the security forces

At about 5:30, people begin firing from the Conservatory building and from the Maidan barricades at the security forces near the Stela. By 9:00, according to data the court considered proven, three members of the security forces had been killed and 39 wounded by firearms. Before 7:30 the cabs of the water cannons were holed by bullets; after 7:55 ambulances arrive one after another.

The court did not establish who fired. These deaths, in its words, “залишилися взагалі не розслідуваними” (remained entirely uninvestigated).

08:30The retreat

At about 8:30 the units near the Stela begin, in the court’s wording, to withdraw “самостійно та стихійно” (on their own initiative and spontaneously) up Instytutska Street. The court rejected the prosecution’s version that in those minutes the MVS leadership decided to open fire on people: those who could have made such a decision were not on trial in this case. It considered it unproven that such an order reached the regiment.

08:55The protesters follow

People climb toward the footbridge with shields, sticks and Molotov cocktails. According to the videos, there are a few armed individuals among them. The retreat is covered by a Berkut group with green markings; its officers fire Fort-500 pump-action shotguns.

08:59:34The first death

Heorhii Arutiunian is among the first to advance. He is killed by a charge of buckshot. The court established that the shot was fired from a shotgun of the group with green markings, “у натовп” (into the crowd) from behind the parapet. The moment was filmed from two points, and the time on the recordings matches to the second.

Katchanovski claims

The three killed before the special company came out were victims of Maidan shooters.

What the check shows (Claude)

In Arutiunian’s case, shots resembling those of the group with green markings can be heard on the video at the same second, and the wound track runs from its direction; the court linked the death to this group.

09:00:37–09:02Two deaths without an answer

A minute later Bohdan Vaida is killed next to the water cannon, and soon Oleksandr Baliuk is mortally wounded. Here the court did not find the shooter. The buckshot entered Vaida from behind and to the left; a ricochet off the water cannon’s cab, which may have been fired at from the protesters’ side, is not ruled out.

For both, Claude puts the odds at almost even: “too close to call.”

09:03–09:05The special company opens fire

Until this minute the Kyiv special company is in its gray bus. At 09:03:05 its officers fire their first shots at the corner of the October Palace. At about 9:05 the deputy regiment commander orders his men to open fire with their rifles to get the security forces, mostly Internal Troops conscripts, out of the palace.

The court called this order “явно злочинним через вимогу її невибіркового застосування небезпечно поруч із скупченням активістів” (manifestly criminal because of the requirement to use it indiscriminately dangerously close to a crowd of activists): they were ordered to fire indiscriminately, dangerously close to a crowd of people. The court dates the start of aimed fire at people to 09:17.

09:07–09:12Five minutes at the footbridge

Maksym Shymko, Bohdan Solchanyk, Andrii Saienko and Bohdan Ilkiv are killed. Ilkiv was shot in the back. The bullet from his body was fired from a rifle assigned to a special company officer.

Vitalii Kotsiuba was killed next to a post, facing the Conservatory; the special company’s positions were far to his left. Here a shot from the Maidan side is the more probable one. The court did not establish where Oleksii Bratushka was wounded.

Katchanovski claims

Ilkiv and Saienko were killed from the hotel: according to him, the steep wound tracks point to this.

What the check shows (Claude)

Both bodies contained bullets from special company barrels. But in Saienko’s case the court itself acknowledged that his posture rules out a shot from the direction of the palace, and this contradiction has not been resolved.

09:17

A special company group at the corner of the October Palace, two seconds from the CNN recording. Two are aiming horizontally to the left; yellow armbands are legible on their sleeves. Next to them is a man in bluish spotted camouflage. In these same minutes Pekhenko, Aksenyn and Moisei are killed on the platform in front of the palace.

Of the weapons next to the special company, the one that fits is the C.TSM .308 of an Omega sniper attached to the Internal Troops’ Western detachment. In Claude’s estimate, it is probably him (about 75%).

At 10:31:50 the shooting dies down.

Katchanovski claims

Chmilenko was killed with a .30-06 hunting bullet, and the security forces had no weapons for it.

What the check shows (Claude)

The experts wrote only that the tip of the 7.62 mm bullet “approximately” matched a .30-06, an American rifle cartridge; the court did not treat the caliber as established. The .308 (7.62×51) is a different cartridge, but it fires a bullet of the same diameter, and the two cannot be told apart by the tip of the bullet. The security forces did have .308 rifles (Omega and SBU snipers). So the shot was probably fired from a rifle rather than from one of the special company’s Kalashnikov assault rifles, but the caliber does not show which side the shooter was on.

11:28–16:50After the lull

Oleksandr Khrapachenko was killed by an aimed shot from the direction of the government quarter; a 2018 investigation expert examination linked a bullet fragment to the rifle of an Omega sniper, but it was not tested in court. Vitalii Smolenskyi was killed on the Maidan itself by a bullet from a special company rifle; at the same time, the court itself doubted whether this spot could be hit from the barricade.

Volodymyr Melnychuk was killed at 16:50. A National Bank camera records a shot by a special company officer at the same second, if the Vysota synchronization is correct, though the court found that he could have been hit by aimed fire from the hotel, but not from the barricade.

Katchanovski claims

Melnychuk was killed from the hotel: his wife (judging by the sound), eyewitnesses and, according to him, a forensic medical expert pointed to it.

What the check shows (Claude)

The court did not accept the specialist’s opinion given at the investigative experiment. But here the argument carries weight, and Claude counts this episode among the disputed ones.

Method

How Claude weighs a single death

No single piece of evidence in this case answers the question “who killed” in full. Each covers its own part, and the strength of a conclusion depends on how many parts converge.

Video: who fired at that second

Dozens of recordings have been aligned to a single clock. If an officer fires at the same second a person falls, that is a strong link. Video does not show where the bullet goes.

Wound and posture: where the shot came from

The wound track and how the person was standing give the direction. If only the Maidan is behind and only the security forces are in front, the direction almost settles the matter. A ricochet or an unknown posture undermines it.

Bullet: from which gun

Only a bullet ties a death to a specific weapon: the expert examination compares the rifling marks with reference test-firings of the rifles. It is the only way to name not just a side but a specific gun.

Here is how this works for three deaths from the same morning. In the first, all three lines converge; in the second, they point to the Maidan side; in the third, they do not converge at all.

That is why ballistics matters so much here. In 12 of the 48 deaths, a bullet from the victim’s body or clothing matched a special company rifle (in two cases it was found in the clothing; in three, the rifle was assigned to an officer who was not at the scene at the time, so someone else fired it).

Each square is one of the dead, in order of time of death. Red with a dot: a bullet from the body matched a special company rifle.

This evidence base has weak points, and Katchanovski targets exactly those. Some physical evidence was lost or excluded, the special company’s rifles were sawn apart, and the chain of custody of a number of bullets is questionable. That is why, even in episodes with direct ballistic evidence, Claude’s estimate for the special company goes no higher than 92%. In the remaining 36 cases, the conclusion rests on video, wounds and the court’s group findings: the court attributed a death to the special company together with other security forces if the shot came from their area during the minutes they were firing.

The other side

How many lives fire from the Maidan side may have taken

The court did not identify any of the shooters on the Maidan side by name. It describes them in terms of groups and roles, and notes that shots at security forces “взагалі ніяк не коментувалися” (were not commented on at all) from the stage and that there was no control over the armed forward groups.

In Claude’s estimate, this fire most likely took the lives of about nine of the 48 protesters, with a reasonable range of 5 to 16. About three of these nine come from four episodes where the Maidan side is more likely, about two from four “too close to call” episodes, and the rest is spread in small shares across other episodes. About 5.4 of the nine are backed by direct indicators of shooting from the Maidan side; the rest is a margin for possible errors in the expert examinations.

A more important question is whether these were accidental hits or deliberate shots at their own people. The shooter’s intent has not been established in any episode. If these nine deaths are distributed among the most likely mechanisms, the result is:

accidentally ≈3.5by mistake ≈1.9deliberately at a protester ≈3.6
miss, ricochet, crossfire ≈3.5mistaken identity ≈1.9shot at a protester ≈3.6

In no episode does the last of these mechanisms get more than half: the highest is Zherebnyi’s, at 46%. Claude puts the probability that at least one such killing took place in a wide range: from 46 to 91%.

If such shots were fired, who could have been behind them? None of the versions rests on an identified shooter, so these are shares of a single uncertainty, not findings.

The dispute

Where Katchanovski is right and where his version does not hold up

The book’s thesis can be tested with a number. He claims that shooters on the Maidan side killed most or almost all of the dead; out of the 48 deaths on February 20, that means 25 or more. Below is what the case record yields, on the same scale.

That is why Claude puts the probability of the book’s full thesis at under 3%. But some of its arguments are correct.

Supported

  • There was shooting from the Maidan side. On February 20, in Claude’s estimate, it was from there that fire to hit people was opened first: from 5:30, at security forces (the court itself considered this fire proven).
  • The court itself pointed to Hotel Ukraina in two deaths, did not rule it out in a third (Pekhenko), and in Melnychuk’s case acknowledged that an aimed shot was possible only from there.
  • The investigation did little ballistic analysis of shot directions, some evidence was lost, and the rifles were destroyed.
  • In six episodes, in Claude’s estimate, the Maidan side is no less likely than security forces.

Not supported

  • In 12 deaths, the bullets matched special company rifles.
  • In about ten deaths, the court used video to link a person’s fall to a shot by security forces in the same second; in six, on one and the same recording.
  • Many of the “steep” wound tracks he relies on run differently in the expert examinations accepted by the court.
  • Weapons that security forces “did not have” turn out to have been in their hands: the 7.62×54R cartridge fits the sniper rifle on the barricade.

How solid this is

The verdict has not entered into force. Most deaths were attributed to the defendants through group findings, without a bullet tied to a specific body. The spread of results is driven mainly by two common risks: that the entire ballistic evidence base is wrong at once, and that all of the court’s group findings are wrong at once. What is unknown

What remains open

  1. Whether the order to fire at people on February 20 came from above the regiment level.
  2. Who exactly fired from the Maidan side, where they stood and whom they were aiming at. The court did not name a single shooter.
  3. Which member of the group with green markings killed Heorhii Arutiunian. An indictment against the commander of the Sevastopol Berkut company and two of its officers was sent to court in October 2024; what has happened to it since is unknown.
  4. Twelve disputed episodes where the data are scarce or point in different directions.

Much would be settled by comparing the bullets from the bodies with activists’ weapons, by questioning people who were in the hotel and the Conservatory that morning, and by investigating the deaths of security officers, which, according to the court, have remained uninvestigated.
